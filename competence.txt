The PI's research record directly covers all three technical pillars of
this proposal, demonstrating both depth and a track record of delivery.

On Information Flow Control (WP2), the PI co-designed LIO, a
floating-label IFC framework that underpins the eBPF-based enforcement
proposed here. A subsequent paper proving the equivalence of coarse- and
fine-grained IFC systems was awarded a Distinguished Paper at POPL 2019.
Earlier work extending LIO to concurrent programs (ICFP 2012) received
the Most Influential Paper Award in 2022, and practical deployments on
the web platform (Hails, OSDI 2012; COWL, OSDI 2014) demonstrate the
PI's ability to take IFC from theory to real systems. The PI's earliest
IFC result used Arrow types to enforce IFC in Haskell (CSF 2007),
directly establishing the foundation for WP1.

On Differential Privacy (WP3), the PI co-developed DPella, a programming
framework for writing differentially private queries with accuracy
guarantees (IEEE S&P 2020; TOPLAS 2021; OOPSLA 2024). A recent result on
accuracy for DP quotients was published at CCS 2025.

As project leader, the PI has successfully managed two concurrent VR
grants and the WASP-funded project on choreographic programming, all
involving PhD supervision and international collaboration. Collaborations
with leading groups at Boston University, Stanford, and UC San Diego
provide complementary expertise in DP, OS security, and secure systems.

The PI has led an independent research group at Chalmers since 2010. The
combination of foundational results, award-winning publications, and
demonstrated deployment experience confirms the PI's competence to lead
this project.
